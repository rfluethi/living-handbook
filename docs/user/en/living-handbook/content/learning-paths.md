# Create learning paths

Somebody joins, and you would rather not say "read the handbook". A learning path is the answer to that: an ordered selection of pages that already exist in your handbook, with a Next button from one to the following. "Read the handbook" becomes "work through these nine pages in this order".

<details>
<summary>Concept: why this is not a course system</summary>

A learning path puts an order on top of pages that are maintained anyway. It copies nothing, it invents no second place for content, and a lesson stays an ordinary handbook page: same address, same navigation, same review status, same access rules.

What this module deliberately is not: a learning management system. There are no lesson texts of its own, no exams, no grades, no certificates. Anybody who needs those is better served by one of the large LMS plugins. What an LMS cannot do, on the other hand, is know when a page was last changed and last reviewed. That is exactly where the next stage will start.

</details>

## Switching it on

Learning paths are off out of the box. Tick the box under **Handbook → Settings → Learning paths**. The handbook menu then gains a **Learning paths** entry.

Switching it off hides everything again and deletes nothing. Your paths, their texts and their lesson lists stay, and they are there unchanged as soon as you tick the box again.

## How it works

1. Open **Handbook → Learning paths → New learning path**.
2. Give it a **title** ("Editorial onboarding"), pick the **handbook** it belongs to on the right, and **save once**. Only then does the path know which handbook to search for lessons in.
3. Write a few sentences into the text area: who the path is for, what should stick at the end, roughly how long it takes. This text is the entry page.
4. In the **Lessons** box, type into the search field. It offers published pages of this handbook. Clicking a result appends it to the list.
5. **Move up** and **Move down** put the list in the right order, **Remove** takes a lesson out again. The buttons work with a mouse and with the keyboard alike.
6. **Publish.**

## What readers see

The entry page of the path shows your text, then the numbered list of lessons and a **Start** button. On every lesson a slim bar appears above the title: the name of the path, "Lesson 3 of 9", and the way to the previous and the next page.

Somebody who has read a few sees those lessons ticked off, "3 of 9 lessons read" at the top, and **Start** becomes **Continue**, which leads straight to the first lesson still open.

Paths are found on the entry page of their handbook: they sit at the top, with the number of lessons. A handbook without paths looks exactly as it did.

> **The progress is in the browser, not on the server.** This first stage stores nothing about individual people: no table, no user field, no cookie. Anybody who clears their browser data or changes device starts over as far as the display goes. The path itself stays, of course. This stage is therefore no proof of who read what, and the interface says so to the reader as well.

## Access

A learning path belongs to exactly one handbook and is as visible as that handbook. For an internal handbook that means: whoever may not read the handbook does not see the path either, neither in the list nor through its address.

Lessons go through the same check. A page somebody may not see appears for that person neither in the list nor in the counter. That makes the path a different length for two people, and it is meant to: the alternative would be to reveal in the counter how much somebody is missing.

## When the handbook changes

There is nothing to maintain on the path. What is stored is a set of references and an order; everything else is worked out when the page is read. A lesson drops out of the list, the numbering and the Next button by itself as soon as it is deleted, set back to draft, or moved to another handbook. Pages that were renamed or moved simply appear with their new title.

What the path does **not** do in this stage: tell anybody that a lesson has changed substantially and should be read again. That is the second stage.

## Limits of this stage

* No progress on the server, so no proof and no overview of who is how far.
* A path can only hold pages from its own handbook.
* At most 200 lessons per path.
* No knowledge questions and no badges.
